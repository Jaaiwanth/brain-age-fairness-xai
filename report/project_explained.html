1The big idea

A brain-age model is an AI that looks at a brain MRI scan and guesses the person's age. Doctors and researchers care about the difference between the guess and the real age: if your brain "looks" 8 years older than you are, that might say something about brain health.

Most projects stop at "how accurate is it?". We asked two harder questions:

  • Is it fair? Is it equally right for men and women, and for people scanned at different hospitals?
  • Is it right for the right reasons? Is it actually reading the brain, or is it partly noticing which machine took the picture?
AnalogyA dog trained to find drugs in suitcases might really be smelling the plastic wrap one airport uses. It would be "right", but for the wrong reason. We checked whether our model is doing something similar with hospital scanners.

2Words you need (glossary)

Bias correctionA standard fix: brain-age models over-guess young people and under-guess old people.
Harmonisation (ComBat)A standard method that removes each hospital's average "shift" from data.

3The data

  • IXI dataset: public brain MRI scans of healthy adults aged 20–86 from three London hospitals: Guys (Philips 1.5T), HH (Philips 3T), IOP (GE 1.5T).
  • 485 people after cleaning, split 339 train / 73 validation / 73 test, the same split for both of our pipelines.
  • 14 scans were excluded: 10 had no record, 2 had no age, 2 had conflicting records.
Important early fixThe first version of our demographics table attached ages to the wrong people (a row number was used as the subject ID). Correct models looked broken. We rebuilt every label from the official IXI.xls. Both of us found this independently, and the public iAudit project agrees with the corrected labels. Everything in this document uses the fixed labels.
NoteIOP recruited younger people (average 41 vs about 48 at the other two). That is why every comparison is adjusted for age.

4The model

SFCN (Simple Fully Convolutional Network, Peng et al. 2021) is a 3D neural network, pretrained on about 14,000 UK Biobank brains. It won a brain-age competition.

  1. The scan is cleaned: skull removed, brightness unevenness corrected, aligned to a standard brain shape, cut to 160×192×160 voxels.
  2. Six layers of filters find patterns, from edges up to "how big are the fluid spaces".
  3. Everything is squeezed into 64 numbers, the model's summary ("notes") of the brain.
  4. A final layer turns those into a probability for each age (18 to 90). The predicted age is the weighted average.

The original could only output ages 42–82, so we gave it a new 18–90 output layer and fine-tuned it in two stages (first the new layer, then the whole network gently). DeepBrainNet and a second SFCN, built in the experiments/ pipeline, serve as independent comparisons.

6Results 1–3: accuracy, fairness, unseen sites

① It is accurate

Before fine-tuning the error was 10.2 years (young people were guessed at about 55). After fine-tuning: 3.71 years on the test set (95% CI 3.10–4.38), correlation 0.966. Published models on small datasets like IXI usually get 3–5 years.

Comparison: on validation, our SFCN 3.90, the second SFCN 3.88, DeepBrainNet 4.12. The two SFCNs are equally good; test numbers must not be used to rank pipelines.

② Fair across sex, but one hospital stands out

Comparison (age-adjusted)Brain-age gapMeaning
Men vs women−0.59 [−2.67, +1.64]No difference ✅
HH vs Guys+1.06 [−1.44, +3.53]No difference ✅
IOP vs Guys−5.06 [−8.66, −1.34]IOP brains look ~5 yrs younger ⚠️
Is the IOP finding real?Yes. 8 of 9 IOP test people have a negative gap; removing any one person keeps it between −4.2 and −5.9; it appears separately in validation (−4.1) and test (−5.1). In training people it is absent (−1.0), so it is a problem with new IOP people.

③ A model that never saw IOP stumbles there

We trained three extra models, each with one hospital left out. Without IOP, error on IOP rose by 3.3 years (6.8 vs 3.6). Without HH, nothing changed. IOP is the hard hospital to generalise to.

7Results 4: the heatmaps

Heatmaps show where the prediction is most sensitive. Ours light up the deep brain areas around the fluid spaces (ventricles), which are known to change with age.

Method note: standard Grad-CAM gave empty maps for 20–80% of people, so we used gradient × activation (HiResCAM-style). This choice was made before looking at any group results.

Two trust tests, both passedSanity check: scramble the model's layers and the heatmaps change completely (similarity only 0.13), so they come from what it learned. Deletion test: blank out the 5% of brain the heatmap ranks highest and the prediction moves 4.8 years; a random 5% moves it only 1.0; the lowest-ranked 5% only 0.5.

Heatmap patterns differ between hospitals, but not between men and women.

8Results 5–7: right for the wrong reasons?

⑤ The model learned the scanner

We took the model's 64 internal numbers and trained a simple program to guess the hospital from them alone.

What the guesser could seeHospital guessed correctly (chance 33%)
Only age and sex41%
The model's 64 numbers94%
The 64 numbers, age and sex removed91%

The model was never told about hospitals, yet its "notes" carry a scanner fingerprint. It was already there in the original UK Biobank model (93%).

The scanner-look test (the key experiment)

We took the same brain and changed only its brightness pattern to match another hospital's typical scan. The anatomy never changed.

  • HH brains given IOP's look: 3.9 years younger, all 45 of 45.
  • IOP brains given HH's look: 3.1 years older, all 18 of 18.
What this provesThe same brain gets a different age depending on how it was scanned. The model is partly right for the wrong reasons.

⑥ But IOP's 5 years are not a simple scanner trick

SuspectTestResult
BrightnessSwap Guys ↔ IOP brightness (age-adjusted)≤ 1 year ❌
BlurIOP scans are much blurrier at the same resolution; blur other brains to IOP's levelNot younger (+0.9 / 0.0) ❌
The fingerprintRemove it with ComBat (hospital recognition 91% → 36%, accuracy unchanged)Offset stays (−4.5 → −4.0) ❌
A careful check that changed a conclusionBecause IOP's volunteers were younger, IOP's "typical look" was first built partly from younger brains. Once we adjusted each hospital's look for age and sex, the apparent IOP effect almost vanished. This is why we always adjust for age.

So what causes it? Either scanner properties we did not test, or IOP's volunteers genuinely differ. IXI cannot separate the two; that would need the same people scanned at several hospitals.

⑦ It knows when it is unsure

The model gives a probability for every age, so it also shows how sure it is. It is more unsure about IOP (+2.5 years), and twice as unsure when it never saw IOP (12.5 vs 6.1), while its "90% range" still contains the true age 92% of the time. It is less accurate there but admits it: not confidently wrong. For single scans the signal is weak; it is a group-level warning.

10The rules we followed

  1. One model trained on everyone; results split by group afterwards.
  2. Say "acquisition-site effects", never "hospital bias".
  3. Never "the model thinks differently"; say attribution patterns differ.
  4. No clinical claims: healthy volunteers, methods audit only.
  5. Control for age in every comparison.
  6. Report confidence intervals everywhere.
  7. Never tune on the test set: it was opened exactly once.

12Questions people will ask

QuestionShort answer
Why SFCN?Competition winner, pretrained on 14k brains, 3D, and its age-probability output gives uncertainty for free.
Is 3.7 years good?Yes; typical published range on small datasets is 3–5 years.
Only 9 IOP test people?Wide range, but it replicates in validation and survives leaving out any one person.
Is IOP's hospital biased?We call it an acquisition-site effect; in IXI, site and the people recruited are mixed up.
Are the heatmaps trustworthy?They passed the randomisation and deletion tests.
Did you tune on the test set?No. Opened once, after every decision was final.
What next?Same-person multi-scanner data; run the audit on DeepBrainNet; train a scanner-blind model.
The final sentence to rememberAccurate and fair across sex, but not scanner-invariant. Brain-age gaps from it should not be compared across scanners without checking first, and the model's own uncertainty tells you where to be careful.
