Right for the wrong reasons? Auditing a brain-age model beyond accuracy.

A deep network that reads a person's age from a brain MRI is accurate to within four years. We asked whether it is accurate for everyone, and whether it is accurate for the right reasons.

Fine-tuning turns a good model into an accurate one

The UK Biobank model can only output ages 42–82, so every young adult is predicted to be about 55. Re-training its last layer for ages 18–90 and fine-tuning the network cuts the error by more than half.

04

The model was only taught age. It learned the scanner too.

Inside the network, every brain becomes 64 numbers before the age is read out. A simple classifier can tell which hospital a scan came from using those numbers alone, even after age and sex are removed. And when we change only the look of a scan to another hospital's, the predicted age moves.

Same brain, different scanner look

Change in predicted age (years) when each site's brains are given another site's intensity profile. Hover a cell.

HH brains given IOP's look: 45 of 45 become younger. IOP brains given HH's look: 18 of 18 become older.

What this shows: the prediction is not invariant to acquisition. The same anatomy can receive a different brain age depending on how it was scanned. This is the "wrong reasons" half of the title.

05

But IOP's five years are not a simple scanner artefact

We tested the three most obvious acquisition explanations at IOP's measured levels. None of them reproduces the offset.

Why the age adjustment mattered: IOP recruited younger volunteers (mean 41 vs 48). Without standardising each site's "typical look" for age, IOP's look seemed to make brains younger ( years). Once adjusted, the effect almost vanished (). What remains possible is either acquisition properties not tested here or genuine cohort differences, and IXI cannot separate the two.

06

It knows when it is unsure

The model outputs a probability for every age, so it also says how certain it is. Its uncertainty is highest for IOP, and it doubles when IOP was never seen, while its 90% range still contains the true age about 9 times in 10. Less accurate, but not confidently wrong.

07

The heatmaps can be trusted

Attribution concentrates on the deep grey matter around the ventricles, which is known to change with age. Two checks: scrambling the model's layers destroys the maps (correlation ), and smoothing away the regions the heatmap ranks highest changes the prediction far more than smoothing random regions.

The verdict

Auditing beyond accuracy changed the answer to "is this a good model?"

Holds up

Accurate and fair across sex

3.7-year error on unseen brains; no sex difference in gap, error or attribution; faithful heatmaps; uncertainty that rises where it should.

Caution

Not acquisition-invariant

It encodes the scanner and its answer shifts with scan appearance alone. Brain-age gaps from it should not be compared across sites without site-aware validation.

Open question

Why IOP?

A robust five-year offset that intensity, blur and the feature fingerprint do not explain. Separating scanner from cohort needs travelling-subject data.
